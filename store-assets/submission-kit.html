LayerLink turns your phone into an instant screen-sharing broadcaster. Start a session, share one link, and anyone can watch your screen live in their browser — no app install, no account, and nothing to sign up for on either side. HOW IT WORKS Tap Initialize Broadcast and LayerLink generates a private session link on the spot. Send it to whoever needs to see your screen — a teammate, a support agent, a friend troubleshooting your phone. The moment they open the link, they're watching live. Under the hood, LayerLink uses WebRTC, the same real-time video technology behind modern video calling — the actual video stream travels directly between your phone and your viewer's browser, peer-to-peer. It never passes through or gets stored on our servers. BUILT FOR REAL USE • Floating Stop control — end a broadcast from anywhere on your phone, even with LayerLink in the background, with a confirmation step so a stray tap can't cut you off mid-session. • One-tap copy and share for the session link. • Works with any modern browser on the viewing end — desktop or mobile. • No account, no login, no persistent history. PRIVACY BY DESIGN LayerLink doesn't record, store, or upload your screen anywhere. The only data that touches our servers is short-lived connection metadata needed to link your phone to your viewer's browser, and it's discarded the moment your session ends. Built by Layerbit AI.

03

Data safety

Play Console → App content → Data safety. Google's exact question wording shifts over time — match these answers to whatever phrasing you're shown, don't expect a verbatim match.

Does your app collect or share any required user data types?
Yes — limited, and only transiently.
Photos and videos
Not collected. This is the one worth getting right: your screen video travels peer-to-peer straight to the viewer's browser over WebRTC. It never reaches LayerLink's or Layerbit's servers, so it isn't "collected" under Play's definition even though sharing video is the whole point of the app.
App activity / Device or other IDs
A randomly generated session ID and WebRTC connection metadata (SDP offer/answer, ICE network candidates) are sent to Firebase Realtime Database only to connect your phone to a viewer's browser. Not tied to any user account or identity. Deleted automatically when the session ends or is stopped.
Location, personal info, financial info, health, messages, audio, files, calendar, contacts, web browsing
Not collected — none of these apply.
Is data encrypted in transit?
Yes — HTTPS to Firebase for signaling; the WebRTC video itself is encrypted end-to-end by protocol (DTLS-SRTP), independent of our signaling channel.
Can users request data deletion?
There's no persistent account or identity to delete data for — session data is anonymous and self-expiring. Worth stating exactly that if the form asks for a data-deletion policy description.
04

Sensitive permissions declaration

Play Console → App content → Permissions declaration. Pick the closest matching category shown in your console, then paste the description.

SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW (display over other apps)
LayerLink shows a small floating control while a screen broadcast is active, so the user can end the session from any app without switching back to LayerLink. It only appears during an active broadcast and is fully user-draggable; tapping it asks for confirmation before stopping the session.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PROJECTION
The foreground service captures and transmits the user's screen in real time — this is the app's core feature, not a background task. It must run as a foreground service with an ongoing notification so Android does not terminate the capture while the user multitasks in other apps during a broadcast.

Content rating questionnaire

Play Console → App content → Content ratings. This is an interactive questionnaire only you can complete, but every honest answer for LayerLink is straightforward:

  • Category Tools / Productivity (not Social or Communication — LayerLink doesn't message, match, or connect users to strangers).
  • Answer "No" to violence, sexual content, gambling, user-generated social content, and drug references — none apply.
  • Answer "No" to sharing location or user-generated content with other users beyond the screen the broadcaster explicitly chooses to share.

Signing key & release build

The one part that must happen on your machine, in Android Studio — a signing key can't be generated remotely without putting your private key somewhere it shouldn't be.

Back this up somewhere safe once created. If you lose this key, you can never publish an update to this same app listing again — Play Console cannot reset or recover it for you.
Generate the key + first release build
  1. Android Studio → Build → Generate Signed App Bundle / APK → choose Android App Bundle.
  2. Click Create new... under Key store path. Choose a save location outside the project folder (so it can never be accidentally committed), set a strong store password and key password, fill in the certificate fields (name/organization — anything reasonable, this isn't shown to users).
  3. Back in the repo, copy keystore.properties.example to keystore.properties (same folder, already git-ignored) and fill in the real storeFile path, storePassword, keyAlias, keyPassword you just set.
  4. Re-run Generate Signed App Bundle — it'll now pick up keystore.properties automatically. Build variant: release.
  5. The signed .aab lands in app/release/app-release.aab — that's the exact file Play Console's "Create new release" screen asks you to upload.
